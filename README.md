# BetterInternet — Draft 3.7

## What's new
- One persistent plan beside the floor map, stacked below on narrow screens. Search results automatically preview in a separate proposal; replacing an existing proposal requires confirmation.
- Numbered proposed changes, linked to an expandable explanation. Improvement map compares the predicted current and proposed selected workload. Green = at least 2 percentage points more end-to-end activity delivered; blue = at least 5 points local improvement without an end-to-end improvement meeting the threshold; red = at least 2 points end-to-end decline or 5 points local decline; red takes precedence. These are model display thresholds, not standards.
- Compare a documented-port search against a broader search including representative room sites. Practical existing-port plans preferred when useful and hypothetical installation costs unknown. A broader plan may be preferred if the practical search finds no meaningful benefit, or if a known quote is included and the broader plan improves score by more than 1.5 points. Both use greedy multi-AP search; four priced UniFi models.
- Optional cabling allowance per hypothetical site counts toward the spending limit; unknown costs excluded and must be verified. A hypothetical site may have an undocumented existing port, so a new line is not asserted. No cable routing, switch capacity, site survey or installed quote is inferred.
- Per-AP explanation compares full proposal to the same proposal with that AP removed, holding other equipment and settings fixed. Individual effects are not additive.
- Proposal equipment price metadata retained in local project and JSON import/export; other search options are in-session and require rerunning the search after reopening.
- No-purchase results appear inline with practical next steps. Existing school-total planner, exam planner, editor and four-corner resizing preserved. Automatic optimization remains additions only; manual permission-aware changes still supported.

## Draft 3.3: planning companion

- The floor plan opens directly. Find Wi-Fi improvements, prepare for exams, or open the school summary without moving through separate mandatory stages.
- Automatic add-only search compares each apparently unused documented Ethernet jack against four priced, manufacturer-linked UniFi models. Select an optional spending limit and up to five new APs. The baseline is a zero-cost option. Existing equipment is never replaced or moved by the optimizer. Unauthorized additions require an explicit hypothetical plan. Select a room for a search that gives its active devices triple weight in the delivered/local workload terms.
- Click a documented network jack in Current to enter availability, power and negotiated link speed. Unavailable and unpowered locations are excluded; known PoE-only jacks exclude PoE+ hardware. Unknown power and port availability require verification; unknown link speed is assumed to be 1 Gbps. New AP channel choices use modeled known overlapping channels. Physical feasibility and compatibility are not independently verified.
- Results identify equipment, locations, cost, changed modeled workload, affected rooms, remaining bottlenecks, and other spending levels. Preview creates a separate proposal; confirmation is required before replacing an existing proposal. No installation, ordering, or configuration is performed. The school internet allocation remains unchanged by the add-only search.
- Prices are editable USD snapshots of the manufacturer's US storefront, including listed surcharge, checked November 26, 2026: U6+ $142, U6 Pro $175, U7 Pro $208, U6 Enterprise $307. Source: https://store.ui.com/us/en/category/wifi-flagship . Not live quotes or stock checks. Taxes, shipping, adapters, switches, installation, controller costs, labor and other expenses are not included unless the user enters an allowance; obtain current quotes before purchasing. The search does not cover every brand/model or find online prices automatically.
- Search ranking: 75% modeled delivered workload, 15% modeled local network workload, 10% client-sampled coverage. At each round all affordable eligible jack/model combinations are tested (subject to 1,200 total trials). Greedy extension prefers a cheaper choice within 0.5 score percentage points of the best candidate. Final selection prefers the least-cost tested plan within 1.5 score points of the best, provided at least 2 score points of improvement over baseline. These thresholds and weights are design choices, not standards. Multi-AP combinations are not exhaustively enumerated; results are not guaranteed globally optimal. Unknown existing channels, cross-floor RF effects, actual interference, roaming, port/switch constraints and client behavior may change outcomes. No real-world speed is guaranteed.
- Exam preparation presents published College Board minimum bandwidth, selected classrooms, modeled local limitations, competing classroom demand, unknown traffic and protection warnings, plus next steps and optional classroom allowance estimates. It is not a certification of exam readiness.

## Models and assumptions

The original Draft 3 RF path-loss and AP airtime model is retained. Radio service uses strongest compatible signal, client generation/streams, assumed channel widths/efficiency and known overlapping channels; full-duplex Ethernet directions are evaluated separately. Missing radio/channel data use representative assumptions. Per-floor capacity calculations are summed; WAN offered demand includes wired and wireless internet-bound traffic, including unserved offered demand, which is conservative for estimating offered WAN load. For each wireless association, the delivered workload fraction is approximated as `min(1,1/max(radio shared airtime ratio, AP uplink ratio))`; unserved wireless clients receive zero; wired traffic has no modeled local switch limit. Each internet-bound direction is then multiplied by `min(1, available WAN / aggregate offered WAN demand)`. Non-internet-bound demand is not reduced by WAN. The resulting value is demand-limited, approximate, and not maximum device throughput, a measured throughput, or a guarantee of fair allocation. This proportional scaling is a simplified congestion approximation and can conservatively compound access and WAN bottlenecks. Unknown WAN capacity is omitted and flagged; unknown outside traffic is not deducted. Actual throughput depends on traffic distribution, QoS, external interference, roaming, retransmissions, latency and other factors not simulated here.

Bandwidth Planner: `remaining = available * (1 - extra allowance) - exam demand - known competing traffic`; exam demand is simultaneous test takers times the published per-student minimum in each direction. Classroom demand is `ceil(devices * fraction actively transferring) * per-active-device demand`. Classroom count is the smaller of download and upload budgets divided by classroom demand (rounded down) when both directions are known. Counts are illustrative ceilings, not permission to keep devices online. Zero extra allowance is the default for the theoretical planner; the capacity dashboard additionally displays a clearly labeled 20% illustrative allowance. No QoS is assumed when unknown; reported QoS is not verified. Follow district authorization and College Board testing instructions.

### Sources vs assumptions

- College Board, [Prepare Your Network](https://bluebook.collegeboard.org/technology/networks/measure-speed-building): SAT/PSAT 0.2 Mbps download and 0.2 Mbps upload per simultaneous test taker; AP 1 Mbps download and 0.2 Mbps upload. The school scenario assumes every modeled device tests; override rooms to represent other instructional activities. Minimums are not continuous traffic measurements.
- YouTube, [System requirements](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/78358?hl=en): recommended 2.5 Mbps for 720p and 5 Mbps for 1080p playback. Video upload allowances and other classroom workloads are illustrative.
- Typical school day: 60% active, 2/0.3 Mbps per active device, 85% internet-bound; light 30%, 0.5/0.1, 85%; heavy 80%, 5/2, 90%. Mixed school day: 40% idle; 25% browsing 0.5/0.1, 20% classroom apps 2/0.3, 10% video 5/0.2, 5% heavy 10/3. The mixed scenario is aggregated into a weighted equivalent active-client rate. These are modeling assumptions, not observed school usage. Room and school overrides are editable. User-entered traffic and speed estimates are not independently measured by BetterInternet.
- Manufacturer-linked AP profiles in the retained equipment library distinguish published hardware specifications from assumed operating values.
